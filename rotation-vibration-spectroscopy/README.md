# Rotation-Vibration Spectroscopy of Molecules

## Overview
This experiment used Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy to investigate molecular rotation-vibration transitions, spectral resolution, and the molecular properties of hydrogen chloride.

## Focus
- FTIR spectrometer calibration using water vapour and polystyrene
- Interferogram acquisition and Fourier transformation
- Spectral resolution, zerofilling, and apodisation
- Optical interference and cuvette gap-width determination
- HCl rotation-vibration spectroscopy
- Molecular force constants, bond lengths, and isotope splitting

## Key Results
- Mean wavenumber calibration deviations: 0.49 cm⁻¹ (water vapour) and 0.78 cm⁻¹ (polystyrene)
- HCl vibrational transition wavenumbers: 2888.50 cm⁻¹ and 2886.44 cm⁻¹
- Molecular force constants: approximately 482 N/m
- Molecular bond lengths: approximately 1.29 × 10⁻¹⁰ m
- Estimated chlorine isotope abundances: 71.5% (³⁵Cl) and 28.5% (³⁷Cl)

## Summary
The experiment demonstrated how interferometric measurements and Fourier analysis produce infrared spectra. The rotation-vibration spectrum of HCl was analysed to determine molecular constants, isotope-dependent spectral features, and the influence of instrumental and processing parameters on spectral quality.
